# Narration guide — rough cut v2

Target delivery: warm, direct, 105–115 words per minute. Record each numbered beat as a separate audio file; short clips are much easier to redo and align than one continuous take. Leave roughly half a second of silence at the start and end of each clip.

## Timed script

**Opening — 00:00–00:17.5**

This is what an AI-generated lesson looks like when it is built for doing, not just reading. I can manipulate the graph, watch the slope update live, and keep learning beside Codex.

**1 — 00:17.5–00:31.5**

This is a tailored, interactive lesson running beside Codex. It was built from my goal, my approved context, and the workflow I was already using—not from a fixed template.

**2 — 00:31.5–00:45.5**

I start with a short brief: what I want to learn, the outcome, my level, the time available, and the teaching modes I prefer. The canvas turns it into a prompt.

**3 — 00:45.5–00:59.5**

I paste that prompt into Codex. Through WebMCP, Codex discovers the learning tools, starts a session, and drives the canvas without sending me into a separate authoring interface.

**4 — 00:59.5–01:17.4**

Before authoring, context stays proposed. I can approve a useful claim, correct an inaccurate one, or leave personal history out. Here I accept one signal and rewrite another. Nothing is used silently.

**5 — 01:17.4–01:35.4**

Codex now builds the lesson progressively. I have sped up this section, but the structure, explanations, evidence, questions, and transfer task all appear in sequence, so the process stays visible.

**6 — 01:35.4–01:55.4**

The lesson is not published automatically. I review the lesson facts, approve the exact revision, and ask Codex to publish it. That explicit gate keeps the learner in control and the workflow auditable.

**7 — 01:55.4–02:17.4**

The result is more than a generated page. It combines concise explanations, diagrams, linked sources, and checkpoints. Because the lesson uses only reviewed signals, examples stay relevant without quietly pulling in private workspace history.

**8 — 02:17.4–02:34.4**

The canvas stays connected while I learn. When a question is unclear, I ask Codex to simplify it. Codex explains the choices, updates the lesson, and saves my answer as structured evidence.

**9 — 02:34.4–02:47.4**

The final transfer task becomes a brief for my next Codex job. I save it and finish with a reusable outcome—not just a completion badge.

## Simplest recording workflow

1. Open this script on a second device or print it so it never appears in the capture.
2. Record the nine beats separately with macOS Voice Memos, QuickTime Player (`File → New Audio Recording`), or Audacity. Use headphones and keep the microphone 10–15 cm from your mouth.
3. Export WAV or AIFF when available; M4A is also fine. Name the files `00-opening` through `09-finish` (keeping their audio extension) and place them in `public/voiceover/`.
4. Send the folder back to Codex. The tracks can then be trimmed, aligned to these timecodes, loudness-normalized, mixed into Remotion, and used to generate captions.

For a free recording/editing alternative in future demos, OBS Studio is the strongest all-in-one option for screen plus microphone. For this already-recorded capture, separate voice clips plus Remotion is the least fragile workflow.
